If you got a fine or infringement notice in Victoria and you disagree with it, or you
can't pay, you have options. Which ones you can use depends on the stage your fine has
reached.

## The stages

A fine moves through stages. First an Infringement Notice, then a Penalty Reminder
Notice, then registration with Fines Victoria, then a Notice of Final Demand, then
enforcement. Going to court only works until the fine is registered. A fine can be
registered once the time to pay on the Penalty Reminder Notice has passed, so do not
wait for a Notice of Final Demand. By then it may be too late. Nominate another driver
as early as you can; the due date is on your notice. After a Notice of Final Demand you
can still ask Fines Victoria for a review, and for help to pay.

## Ask for a review

You can ask for a review on one of five grounds. These are: you did not know about the
fine; the decision was against the law; it was a case of mistaken identity; there were
exceptional circumstances; or there were special circumstances. Special circumstances
include a mental illness or disability, a serious addiction, homelessness, or family
violence. If you only just found out about the fine, ask as soon as you can, because a
short time limit applies to that ground.

## Other options

You can nominate the person who was actually responsible, ask to have the matter heard in
court, or ask Fines Victoria for a payment plan or a Work and Development Permit if you
are in hardship. If you go to court, the court can dismiss the charge. If you are found
guilty, it can record a conviction, order you to pay more or less than the fine, and
order you to pay costs.

Excessive-speed, drink-driving and drug-driving fines work differently. There is no
review. You object in writing within 28 days of the date printed on the notice. If you
do not object in time, the notice takes effect as a conviction and you lose your licence
for a time. Paying does not stop this. Get free legal help quickly.

## Get free help

Victoria Legal Aid's Legal Help line is 1300 792 387. The National Debt Helpline gives
free financial counselling on 1800 007 007.
