## What counts as special circumstances?

Special circumstances means one of these was true for you when the offence happened:

- You had a **mental or intellectual disability or illness**
- You had a **serious addiction**
- You were **homeless**
- You were affected by **family violence**
- You are in **long-term circumstances**, not only about money, that make it
  impracticable to pay or deal with the fine

The rule also asks for a link. For an illness, disability or addiction, it must have made it much harder for you to understand that what you did was an offence, or to control what you did. For homelessness or family violence, it must have made it much harder for you to control what you did. That link is the part applications most often leave out. If family violence is involved, Fines Victoria also has a separate Family Violence Scheme you can ask about. Check the current wording with Fines Victoria or a free legal service.

## What evidence might help?

You will generally need to show something that backs up your claim. Useful documents can include:

- A **letter or report from a doctor, caseworker, or support service** that relates to your situation
- The **original fine or notice**, including its infringement number
- Any **letters showing what stage your fine has reached**

Acting early is important — what you can do depends on the stage your fine has reached, so it helps to start before your fine is **registered with Fines Victoria**. That can happen as soon as the time to pay on the Penalty Reminder Notice has passed, before any Notice of Final Demand arrives.

## Who reviews a special circumstances claim?

You apply to the agency that issued the fine, or to **Fines Victoria**. After a review, the agency must tell you the outcome in writing.


## Where can I get free help?

You do not have to deal with this alone. Free help is available:

- **Fines Victoria** — (03) 9200 8111
- **Victoria Legal Aid** — Legal Help line 1300 792 387
- **Victorian Aboriginal Legal Service (VALS)** — 1800 064 865 (for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people)
- **National Debt Helpline** — 1800 007 007 (free financial counselling)
