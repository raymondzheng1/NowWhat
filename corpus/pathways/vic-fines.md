Victorian fines move through stages: an Infringement Notice, then a Penalty Reminder
Notice, then registration with Fines Victoria, then a Notice of Final Demand, then
enforcement. Some options — like going to court or nominating another driver — are only
available until the fine is registered. Registration comes BEFORE the Notice of Final
Demand: it can happen once the time to pay on the Penalty Reminder Notice has passed.
The exact date to pay is the due date printed on your own notice, so check the notice
itself.

**Court-hearing QA against the Acts, 2026-10-31.** Every court-hearing statement was
checked against the authorised versions on legislation.vic.gov.au — Infringements Act
2006 (v067), Fines Reform Act 2014 (v035), Road Safety Act 1986 (v237), Criminal
Procedure Act 2009 (v115) — and Victoria Legal Aid (updated 19 May 2026). Corrected: the
deadline for court and for review is REGISTRATION (IA ss 16(1), 22(2)(a)), which comes
before the Final Demand (FRA ss 16(2), 23(1)) — the old 'that is, before a Notice of
Final Demand' told people they had time they may not have; excessive-speed,
drink-driving and drug-driving fines use a written objection within 28 days of the
notice date or become a conviction (RSA s 89A(2), (4)-(5)), and internal review does not
apply to them (IA s 21(1)(a)); the 'unaware' ground needs non-personal service and a
refusal leaves 14 days (IA ss 22(1)(d), 25(6)-(7)); the Children's Court test is age at
the offence (IA s 3(1)); and the court's outcomes and costs are now stated and sourced
(VLA; CPA ss 401, 403). The 28-day figure is stated as prose only — `deadlineDays` stays
null and no date is computed — pending the supervising lawyer's confirmation.
