id: standing
name: Standing
plainName: Whether you are the right person to bring it
oneLine: If the decision was about you, this is almost never the problem.
order: 40
appliesTo:
  - any
whatItMeans: >-
  Before anyone looks at your case, there is a question of whether you are the
  right person to raise it. This is called standing. If the decision was made
  about you, and it affects your money, your home, your payment or your
  licence, you will normally have it. The question really matters for people
  further from the decision, such as a group objecting on behalf of others.
whatItIsNot: >-
  It is not a test of how strong your case is. It only asks whether you are the
  right person to bring it. Someone can have standing and still not succeed, and
  someone can care deeply and still not have standing.
keyPoints:
  - >-
    If the decision was about you and affects you, standing is rarely the
    obstacle.
  - The test is not the same for every route.
  - >-
    At a tribunal, the law for your decision usually says who may apply. It is
    often anyone whose interests are affected.
  - >-
    Interests are read broadly. They are not limited to money or property, and
    they can be affected indirectly.
  - >-
    For a court, a general concern about government is not enough on its own.
options:
  - name: Merits review at a tribunal
    plainName: Applying to a tribunal
    whatItDoes: >-
      The law that created the review right usually says who may apply. Often
      that is a person whose interests are affected by the decision.
    note: >-
      Interests are read widely. They are not limited to money or property, and
      it does not matter that someone else is affected too.
  - name: The statutory route to a federal court
    plainName: A person aggrieved
    whatItDoes: >-
      Asks whether you are a person aggrieved. This is not read narrowly.
    note: >-
      It means something beyond what any member of the public has. Harm to your
      economic interests can be enough.
  - name: Asking a court to wipe out or stop a decision
    plainName: A real effect on you
    whatItDoes: >-
      Asks whether the decision has a sufficient effect on your legal rights or
      interests.
    note: Damage to a personal or business reputation can count.
  - name: Asking a court to make someone do their duty
    plainName: A duty owed to you
    whatItDoes: >-
      Asks whether you have a sufficient interest, or a special legal right, in
      seeing a public duty carried out.
    note: The starting question is whether a duty is genuinely left undone.
  - name: Asking for an order or a statement of your position
    plainName: A special interest
    whatItDoes: >-
      Asks for a special interest in the subject matter — more than an ordinary
      member of the public has.
    note: >-
      A purely emotional or intellectual concern is not enough on its own. An
      order to do or stop something needs a legal right.

The mind map's standing node, which it ties to the remedy sought: "Standing to bring judicial
review proceedings and the jurisdiction of the 'reviewing' court to hear the application will
depend on a number of factors, including the remedy or remedies sought."

Written to defuse rather than to gate. Our readers are the paradigm case of a person with standing
— the decision is about them and affects their money or their home — so the node opens by saying
so, in `oneLine` and again in the first `keyPoint`. Leading with the tests would invent an anxiety
about a threshold they clear automatically.

The remedy-specific tests are kept, because that is the structure the mind map draws, but they are
labelled by what a person is asking for ("asking a court to make someone do their duty") rather
than by the remedy's Latin name. Someone scanning this should be able to find their own situation
without first learning which writ it corresponds to.
