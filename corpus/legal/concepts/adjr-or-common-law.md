whatItMeans: >-
  For an Australian Government decision there is more than one way to ask a
  court to check it. One is a statute made for the job. It is the more
  straightforward route. The other is the court's older, wider power. Each
  reaches slightly different things. Which one fits is a question for a lawyer,
  not something you have to work out.
whatItIsNot: >-
  This is not a choice you need to make before you get help. You do not have to
  name a route to ask a free legal service for advice. It is here so the
  question makes sense when someone asks it.
keyPoints:
  - There is more than one way to bring a Commonwealth decision to a court.
  - The statutory route is described as the more straightforward one.
  - >-
    It covers a decision of an administrative character made under an Act. It
    also covers some conduct leading up to a decision, and a failure to decide
    when there was a duty to.
  - >-
    Some decisions are carved out of it, including decisions of the
    Governor-General.
  - >-
    The wider route reaches things the statutory one does not, such as powers
    that do not come from an Act, and the validity of rules and regulations.
options:
  - name: The Administrative Decisions (Judicial Review) Act 1977 (Cth)
    plainName: The statutory route
    whatItDoes: >-
      Lets a federal court review a decision of an administrative character made
      under an Act. It is the more straightforward and less technical route.
    note: >-
      It does not reach every Commonwealth decision. Some are excluded by the
      Act itself.
  - name: Section 39B of the Judiciary Act 1903 (Cth)
    plainName: The wider route
    whatItDoes: >-
      A broader power. It reaches powers that do not come from an Act, and the
      validity of rules and regulations.
    note: >-
      It is not limited in the same way as the statutory route, but it is more
      technical.

    explains: >-

      along the way.

The mind map's "AD(JR)" versus "Common Law" split.

Deliberately written so the reader does NOT have to choose. An ordinary applicant never picks a
jurisdictional route; their lawyer does. The node exists so that when a free legal service asks
which Act the decision was made under, the question does not sound like an evasion.

The Hypo's own description of the ADJR route as "more straightforward and less technical" is
carried through, as are the three advantages it lists for s 39B: it "allows review of non-statutory
executive powers (unlike the ADJR Act)", "allows review of the validity of delegated/subordinate
legislation", and "is not limited to matters involving 'an officer of the Commonwealth'".

Scoped to `Cth`. The Victorian equivalent — Order 56 versus the Administrative Law Act 1978, which
also cannot be joined — is carried on the judicial-review process page instead, where the Victorian
bodies already live.
