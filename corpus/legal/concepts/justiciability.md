plainName: Some decisions a court will not look at
oneLine: A few kinds of government action sit outside what a court will review.
order: 10
appliesTo:

whatItMeans: >-
  Courts review government decisions. But not every act of government is the
  kind of thing a court will rule on. Some choices belong to the elected
  government, not to a judge. What matters is the subject matter, not where the
  power came from. Everyday decisions made under an Act are the ordinary case,
  and courts do review those.
whatItIsNot: >-
  This is not a hurdle for ordinary decisions. A Centrelink debt, a fine, a
  housing refusal or a licence decision is made under an Act, and that is
  exactly the kind of decision courts review every day. This narrow rule is
  about things like going to war.
keyPoints:
  - Decisions made under an Act are the normal case, and they are reviewable.
  - >-
    A small group of powers sits further from review — things like foreign
    relations, national security, or a decision to go to war.
  - >-
    What counts is the subject matter of the power, not the label on where it
    came from.
  - >-
    Courts are more willing to step in where the action directly affects one
    person's rights or interests.
  - >-
    They are less willing where the action is political in nature and affects
    the public at large.

The gate the owner's mind map places before judicial review.

Written to reassure rather than to warn. Our readers face decisions made under a statute, which
are squarely reviewable; the doctrine bites on prerogative and high-political action. A node that
opened by listing what courts refuse to touch would frighten people out of a path they are
entitled to, so `whatItIsNot` does the heavy lifting and names their own situation as the ordinary
case.

The Notes give the two application factors verbatim: courts are more likely to intervene where
"the administrative action directly affects an individual's interests, rights or liabilities", and
"Most non-statutory, administrative actions of a senior governmental or political nature, which
tend to affect the public in general, will not be justiciable."
