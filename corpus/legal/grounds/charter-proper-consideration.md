name: Human rights were not properly considered (Victoria)
plainName: They did not properly think about your human rights
oneLine: In Victoria, a public authority has to give real thought to your human rights.
whatItMeans: >-
  Victoria has a Charter of human rights. It binds public authorities in
  Victoria. It asks two things of them. They must not act in a way that is not
  compatible with your human rights. They must also give proper consideration to
  any human right the decision affects. Proper consideration means real thought,
  not a box ticked.
plainExample: >-
  A public body decides where you will live. Nothing in the reasons shows anyone
  thought about what that would mean for your family.
whatRelates:
  - A Victorian public authority made the decision.
  - >-
    The decision affects something personal — your home, your family, your
    privacy, or how you were treated.
  - The reasons never mention your rights at all.
  - The reasons mention your rights, but only as a heading.
  - A right was limited, and the reasons do not explain why that was needed.
whatItIsNot: >-
  This is not a case you win on its own. Breaking this duty is unlawful, but by
  itself it does not automatically make the decision invalid. It usually runs
  alongside another ground rather than replacing one. It also applies only in
  Victoria.

test: >-
  A public authority must not act incompatibly with a human right, and must give
  proper consideration to a relevant human right. Proper consideration is a
  higher standard than the ordinary duty to take a relevant matter into account.

The Combined MR/JR Framework lists "(Vic) Charter s 38" among the grounds of review, and the JR
Hypo sets out the four steps proper consideration requires: understand which rights may be
relevant and how they would be interfered with; seriously turn your mind to the impact on the
person; identify the countervailing interests; and balance the two. The app had no Charter
content at all before this entry.

Two deliberate limits on how this is presented:

**It is scoped to Victoria** via `jurisdictions: [Vic]`, because the Charter does not reach
Commonwealth decisions. Showing it to somebody challenging a Centrelink decision would send them
looking for a protection they do not have.

**`whatItIsNot` carries the consequence rule**, because it is the part most likely to mislead.
Per *Certain Children*, a breach of the s 38(1) duty is unlawful but "does not of itself give
rise to charter invalidity and in relation to a decision, does not constitute jurisdictional
error". Someone reading only the name of this ground would reasonably assume the opposite.
