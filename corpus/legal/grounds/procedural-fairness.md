---
id: procedural-fairness
name: Procedural fairness (natural justice)
plainName: You weren't given a fair chance
oneLine: A fair hearing from a decision-maker who doesn't have a closed or biased mind.
whatItMeans: >-
  Before a decision that affects you, the decision-maker usually has to tell you
  what is being considered and give you a real chance to respond. They also have
  to be free of bias — they should not have a personal interest in the outcome
  or have made up their mind in advance.
plainExample: >-
  A payment is cancelled because of a report you were never shown and never had
  a chance to answer.
whatRelates:
  - You were not told what the decision would be about before it was made.
  - You were not given a real chance to explain your side.
  - Information was used against you that you never got to see or respond to.
  - >-
    The decision-maker seemed to have decided before hearing from you, or had a
    personal stake.
whatItIsNot: >-
  It does not mean you were entitled to win — only that you were entitled to a
  fair process.
usedIn:
  - judicial-review
test: A decision-maker must give a person a fair hearing and be free of bias.
elements:
  - id: hearing
    name: Fair hearing
    layPrompt: >-
      Were you told what was being decided and given a real chance to respond
      before the decision was made?
  - id: bias
    name: No bias
    layPrompt: >-
      Did the decision-maker seem to have made up their mind already? Or did
      they have a personal interest?
sources:
  - Victoria Legal Aid — legalaid.vic.gov.au
status: verified
leadingCases:
  - name: Kioa v West (1985) 159 CLR 550
    pinpoint: Brennan J at 629
    explains: >-
      Some information may count against you. If it is credible and it
      matters, you usually get to see it first.
  - name: Ebner v Official Trustee in Bankruptcy (2000) 205 CLR 337
    pinpoint: 'at [6]'
    explains: >-
      The bias test: would a fair-minded observer reasonably think the
      decision-maker might not be impartial?
  - name: >-
      SZBEL v Minister for Immigration and Multicultural and Indigenous Affairs
      (2006) 228 CLR 152
    pinpoint: 'at [26]-[29]'
    explains: >-
      You are entitled to know the issues the decision may turn on, so you can
      respond to them.
